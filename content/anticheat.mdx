# Anti-Cheat & Security

LACORE ships with a **built-in, multi-layered anti-cheat** — protection is included with the resource.
There is no separate product to buy and nothing extra to wire up: enable it, set your punishment
levels, and your server is defended out of the box.

<Callout type="info">
  Protection is **on the house** — the anti-cheat is part of LACORE, not an add-on. It runs alongside
  everything else and stays fully standalone.
</Callout>

## What you're protected against

LACORE actively guards your server against the common ways cheaters ruin a roleplay experience:

- 🛡️ **Invincibility & health/armour tampering** — god-mode and impossible-stat exploits
- 🔫 **Illegal weapons & combat abuse** — banned weapons, aimbot-style behaviour and rapid-fire abuse
- 💥 **Explosion, projectile & crash exploits** — including screen-crash and lag attacks
- 🏃 **Movement cheats** — teleporting, speed and no-clip
- 🧨 **Mod menus & script injection** — known menu markers and injected-event abuse
- 📦 **Resource dumping** — headless "download-only" clients and dump bots
- ♻️ **Event spam** — mass-action floods (e.g. mass-cuff / dispatch spam), even from a compromised account
- 🕵️ **Anti-cheat tampering** — attempts to disable or strip out the protection itself

## It's on from the start — watching, not swinging

LACORE ships with the anti-cheat **enabled in observe mode**: every check runs, every hit is logged
and scored, and **nobody is kicked or banned**. Each entry records what it *would* have done.

That is deliberate. Tuning thresholds needs data, and gathering data used to mean going live and
risking a wrong ban on your own players — so most servers left the protection switched off, which
protects nobody. Observe mode removes the trade-off: it is safe to run untouched from day one.

Run it for a week. Watch what your own admins trip and how your busiest firefights look. When the log
stops surprising you, set `Mode = "enforce"` in `configs/cfg-anticheat-sh.lua` and the same checks
start acting on what they find.

<Callout type="info">
  Repeat offenders are tracked **across reconnects**. A player who collects flags, disconnects and
  comes back does not start from zero — but points also fade on their own during clean play, so an
  honest player who tripped something once is clear again in minutes.
</Callout>

## Why it's effective

<Callout type="info">
  For security reasons we **don't publish exactly how each check works** — that would only help
  cheaters adapt. What matters for you is the design below.
</Callout>

- **Server-authoritative core.** The most important checks run **on the server**, so a cheater can't
  simply switch off the protection on their own machine.
- **Built for fair play.** A smart, self-balancing system means legitimate players going about normal
  roleplay are **not** punished for false alarms — real offenders are caught, honest players aren't.
- **Escalating, not trigger-happy.** Repeat offenders are handled decisively while one-off blips fade
  away, so you avoid the "banned my whole community by accident" trap.
- **Full control.** Every protection is **configurable** — choose log / kick / ban per detection, and
  whitelist your staff so admins are never caught by their own tools.
- **Evidence & logging.** Detections are logged to your Discord with the player's details (and can even
  attach a screenshot), so your team has a clear audit trail.
- **Context, not just a verdict.** Every flag carries a short log of what that player was doing in the
  seconds before it — respawns, duty changes, getting in and out of vehicles, weapon switches, health
  jumps, earlier flags. That is usually what separates "cheat" from "someone's revive script".
- **Constantly hardened.** The protection is maintained and expanded with every LACORE update.

## What happened just before the flag

A detection code tells you *what* tripped. `GOD_HEALTH · hp=300` reads like a cheat — until you see
that the same player was revived twice forty seconds earlier, at which point it reads like a bug in a
revive script. So each flag arrives with the story around it:

```
**Anticheat: GOD_HEALTH**
Player: SomePlayer (ID 12)
Action: log
Detail: server hp=300 (> 200)

**Leading up to it**
-42s · duty · on duty as Fire/EMS / AMR
-38s · spawn · respawned / revived (immunity window granted)
-21s · vehicle · entered a vehicle (model 1171614426)
-12s · health · health 100 → 300
- 3s · flag · GOD_HEALTH → log (server hp=300)
```

The buffer holds the last 40 lines or 90 seconds, whichever runs out first, and records **changes
only** — a player standing still writes nothing to it. You can also read it for anyone online without
waiting for a flag:

```
/lacore accontext 12
```

<Callout type="info">
  **It stays on your server.** The buffer lives in memory, is dropped the moment the player
  disconnects, and is attached only to the Discord log you were already receiving. It is deliberately
  **not** added to the dashboard report — that payload is documented line by line in
  `configs/cfg-anticheat-sh.lua`, and quietly widening it would be the wrong way to add a feature.
  Turn the buffer off entirely with `Anticheat.Context.enabled = false`.
</Callout>

## Configuration reference — `configs/cfg-anticheat-sh.lua`

<Callout type="info">
  The **thresholds, honeypot names and blacklists are not in this file.** They live in
  `modules/anticheat/anticheat-params-{sh,sv}.lua` so the escrow build can obfuscate them, and so the
  server-only half never reaches a client at all — the config used to ship as plain text, which handed
  every reader the exact numbers to stay under. You can still override any of them here: whatever you
  set wins, the defaults only fill what you leave out.
</Callout>

### Master switches

| Setting | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Enabled` | `true` | The whole anti-cheat. |
| `Mode` | `"observe"` | `"observe"` = every check runs, every hit is logged and scored, **nothing** is kicked or banned; each line says what it *would* have done. `"enforce"` = the actions below are carried out. |

### Actions

Every detection takes one of four actions:

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| `"log"` | Discord webhook only. |
| `"trust"` | Webhook + trust points — escalates only if the player keeps at it. |
| `"kick"` | Webhook + kick. |
| `"ban"` | Webhook + permanent ban. |

In observe mode all four behave as `"log"`.

### Detections

Server-side checks cannot be patched out by a client; client-side ones can, which is why their default
actions are milder.

| Detection | Side | Default | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Explosions` | server | `ban` | |
| `WeaponDamage` | server | `kick` | |
| `EntitySpam` | server | `kick` | `skipOnFramework = true` skips the guard when ESX/QBCore/QBox is detected — garages and vehicle shops spawn server-side and would false-positive. Leave entity policing to the framework's own AC there. |
| `GodMode` | client | `kick` | |
| `BlacklistedWeapons` | client | `ban` | |
| `SpeedHack` | client | `kick` | |
| `Teleport` | client | `log` | Log only on purpose — the core teleports legitimately (spawn, jail). |
| `Invisible` | client | `kick` | |
| `NoClip` | client | `kick` | |
| `SuperJump` | client | `kick` | |
| `BlacklistedVehicles` | client | `kick` | `delete = true` also removes the vehicle straight away. |
| `BlacklistedPeds` | server | `kick`, **off** | God peds / animals used for exploits. The server reads the ped model, so client-swapped models are caught even with the client AC gone. Empty by default — add your own. |
| `Heartbeat` | both | `kick` | The client checks in every 30 s. Silence means the anti-cheat client was stopped (`stop lacore`, thread kill). |
| `Honeypots` | server | `ban` | Event names that ESX/QBCore menus and known cheat menus fire blindly. LACORE is standalone, so **no** legitimate client ever triggers these — a trigger means an injected script. |
| `EventRateLimit` | server | `kick` | Per-event ceiling for abuse-prone core events (mass cuff, jail flood). Still applies when the player *is* authorised — a compromised LEO client is the case it exists for. |
| `AntiDump` | server | `kick` | Recognises dump bots by behaviour: headless clients never run scripts, and dump sessions are short. A random per-session challenge means replaying a static handshake isn't enough. What actually protects your files is the escrow build; this only recognises the behaviour. |

### Server-authoritative sweep

The server itself reads each player's ped health, position and model every interval. Requires OneSync
(Infinity); the module auto-disables and warns if OneSync is off.

| Setting | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ServerSweep.enabled` | `true` | |
| `ServerSweep.teleport.enabled` | `true` | Position half of the sweep. |
| `ServerSweep.action` | `"trust"` | Feeds the trust score rather than banning outright. Tune before switching to `"kick"` / `"ban"`. |

### Combat integrity

Closes the "client anti-cheat was patched out" gap for weapons. All server-side
(`weaponDamageEvent`, `startProjectileEvent`, `ptFxEvent`).

| Setting | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Combat.enabled` | `true` | |
| `Combat.serverBlacklistWeapons` | `ban` | If a player *deals* damage with a blacklisted weapon, the server cancels it and punishes — even if the client check was removed. |
| `Combat.aimbot` | `trust` | Aimbot / silent-aim / triggerbot **heuristic** — never exact, so it feeds trust by default and a hot gunfight can't insta-ban an innocent player. |
| `Combat.projectiles` | `kick` | Grenade / RPG floods and crash projectiles. |
| `Combat.ptfx` | `kick` | Particle-FX spam used by screen-crash and lag exploits. |

### Trust, whitelist & evidence

| Setting | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Trust.enabled` | `true` | Every detection adds points by severity; points **decay** during clean play. Cross a threshold and it escalates. This is what makes `"log"` / `"kick"` safe to run: a single false positive can never ban on its own. |
| `Whitelist.permission` | `"lacore.ac.bypass"` | Any player with this ACE permission is never punished — detections are still logged. Grant it with `add_ace group.admin lacore.ac.bypass allow`. Discord **Staff** / **Dev** roles are bypassed built-in. |
| `Evidence.enabled` | `false` | On a serious detection, request a screenshot from the flagged client and attach the URL to the Discord log. Needs `screenshot-basic` and an upload endpoint. |
| `Evidence.uploadUrl` | `""` | Your own upload endpoint. |
| `Evidence.onSeverity` | `"alert"` | Capture at this severity and above (`info` < `warn` < `alert`). |

### Reporting flags to your dashboard

Off by default, and deliberately so: this sends flags — which are **accusations about individual
players, with their identifiers** — out of your server to the LACORE backend so you can review them
in the dashboard instead of scrolling a Discord channel.

What leaves your server per flag: the detection code, severity, the action taken, the current trust
score, the player's name and identifiers, the server, and a timestamp. Nothing else — no chat, no
positions, no screenshots. Flags keep going to your own database and Discord webhook exactly as
before; this is an **additional copy, never a move**.

| Setting | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Report.enabled` | `false` | Requires an active licence key (`set lacore_license_key`) — that is what attributes flags to your dashboard account. Without one this is inert. |
| `Report.minSeverity` | `"warn"` | `"info"` / `"warn"` / `"alert"` — quieter than the log by default. |
| `Report.batchSec` | `30` | Flush at most every N seconds, so a flag storm sends one batch. |
| `Report.maxBatch` | `50` | Hard cap per flush; the rest is dropped, never queued forever. |

### Bypasses

- The convar `lacore_devmode` on (`1` / `true` / `yes`) turns the anti-cheat **fully off**.
- Discord role **Staff** or **Dev**, or the `Whitelist.permission` ACE, is never punished — but is
  still logged.

## Reviewing flags in your dashboard

Detections always land in **your** database and **your** Discord webhook. If you would rather review
them as a filterable table than by scrolling a channel, you can also have them copied to your LACORE
dashboard under **Anticheat**: what tripped, what the system did about it, the player's running score,
and which players keep coming back.

<Callout type="warning">
  This is **off by default and stays off until you turn it on**, because a flag is an accusation about a
  named player. Switching it on sends the detection, the action taken and that player's identifiers to
  your dashboard account. It never *moves* anything — your own database and Discord log keep receiving
  everything exactly as before.
</Callout>

Turn it on with `Anticheat.Report.enabled = true` in `configs/cfg-anticheat-sh.lua`. The server needs an
active licence key, since that is what attributes the flags to your account. Whitelisted hits are shown
too, marked as such — an admin repeatedly tripping a check is the clearest signal that a threshold wants
adjusting.

### Tell it when it was wrong

Any flag can be marked a **false positive**, and the page turns those marks into the number that
actually helps: the share of each detection you have called wrong. One mislabelled row is noise — a
detection sitting at 40% is a threshold to raise, and you can raise it yourself in the config, where
your value always wins over the default.

<Callout type="info">
  Marks are reversible and never leave your account. They are a tuning signal for **your** server, not
  a vote on anyone else's.
</Callout>

## Recommended server hardening

The anti-cheat is strongest when paired with a few standard FiveM server settings. LACORE checks these on
start-up and reminds you in the console if any are missing — so you always know your server is locked
down. Add these to your `server.cfg`:

```ini
sv_scriptHookAllowed 0      # block ScriptHookV trainers (Menyoo, Simple Trainer, …)
sv_pureLevel 2              # accept only original client files (2 = strictest)
sv_enforceGameBuild 3258    # pin one game build so old-build exploits can't connect
sv_filterRequestControl 4   # filter entity-control requests (stops foreign-entity manipulation)
```

(Plus OneSync enabled, which the server-authoritative checks rely on.)

<Callout type="warning">
  No anti-cheat on any platform can promise to stop **100 %** of cheats — that's simply not how FiveM
  works. LACORE's job is to make cheating **hard, risky and quickly caught**, and to keep your honest
  players safe from false bans. Combined with the recommended server settings above, your community is
  well protected.
</Callout>
