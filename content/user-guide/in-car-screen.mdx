# In-Car Screen

The dashboard screen of your patrol vehicle, running the **LAPD Mobile Client** — the same terminal the MDT key opens, not a cut-down copy of it. It fills itself in as calls come in, it follows what you are doing in the window, and once you run **`/cad`** the camera leans in and hands you the mouse so you can work it.

It is drawn **per client**, not per vehicle: your partner sees their own terminal signed in as themselves, and a civilian sitting in the same car sees the model's own texture, untouched.

> 📑 **Related:** [Using the MDT](/user-guide/mdt) · [MDT Toolbar & Status](/user-guide/mdt-toolbar) · [Working with Incidents](/user-guide/mdt-incidents) · [Commands & Keybinds](/commands)

---

## 👮 Who gets a screen

Your server owner picks the audience in `configs/cfg-vehiclescreen-sh.lua`:

| `VehicleScreen.audience` | Who sees a terminal |
|--------------------------|---------------------|
| `"leo"` *(default)* | Law Enforcement on duty |
| `"duty"` | Anyone on duty — LEO or Fire/EMS |
| `"all"` | Everyone, civilians included (mostly useful for testing) |

Off duty, in a vehicle with no entry in the config, or outside the audience: the screen stays the model's own and `/cad` tells you there is nothing here.

## 🖥 What is on it

The whole [Mobile Client](/user-guide/mdt) — the ribbon, the incident panel, calls, units, query, everything the MDT key gives you. It fills the screen instead of sitting in a corner window, but nothing is removed and nothing is rearranged: one terminal, one set of habits, whether you are looking at the dashboard or at the window.

It also **follows the window you have open**. Which tab you are on, which incident you picked, light or dark: all of that is decided by clicking inside the UI and never travels through the game, so the screen used to sit on Home while you worked in Calls. The open window now reports its view and the screen follows it — one direction only, the screen never argues back.

> ℹ️ **It is the real CAD, not a preview.** A status set on the dashboard is the status dispatch sees; attaching here attaches you for real.

### Why it is bigger than the terminal in your window

The Mobile Client is drawn at real pixel sizes for a monitor — 9px labels, 12px values. Perfectly legible in its own window. On a dashboard it is not, and the reason is three scalings stacked rather than a shortage of resolution:

1. the terminal is drawn at monitor pixel sizes;
2. the browser renders it into a **texture** (864 × 1450 by default);
3. that texture is shown on the screen mesh, which while you are leaned in occupies roughly 509 × 854 pixels of a 1080p monitor — so a 9px label arrives as about **5**, before the mipmap and the game's antialiasing get to it.

Making the texture bigger does not help; it only makes step 3 worse. So the screen lays the terminal out at the size it was *designed* for and spends the surplus texture pixels on scale instead: the terminal renders **zoomed**, and that 9px label lands at about 7.7 monitor pixels — what it looks like in the window.

Your owner can push it further or turn it off with `VehicleScreen.zoom`. It is a trade: **more zoom means bigger text and fewer call rows on screen**, because the terminal is being laid out in a smaller box.

### The alternative: a stripped dashboard terminal

If the full CAD is more than you want on a windscreen, `VehicleScreen.view = "incar"` swaps it for a much smaller screen — active incident, the call list, who else is out, and a status dock along the bottom that is on every tab:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1-ADAM-15  LAPD    ENROUTE 21:47 │
│ ┌────────┬────────┬────────┐     │
│ │ ACTIVE │ CALLS 4│ UNITS 5│     │
│ └────────┴────────┴────────┘     │
│ ┃ #4821   ASSIGNED   CODE 3      │
│ ┃ 459 - BURGLARY IN PROGRESS     │
│ ┃ 1234 Grove Street · POSTAL 1337│
│ ┃ Caller reports two suspects…   │
│ ┃ [1-ADAM-15] [1-DAVID-3]        │
│                                  │
│ [ GPS ][CODE 6][BACKUP][DETACH]  │
│ ────────────────────────────────  │
│ [CLEAR][ENROUTE][CODE 6][BUSY]   │
└──────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Tab | What it shows |
|-----|---------------|
| **ACTIVE** | The call you have open: incident number, state, response code, type, address and postal, the original message, every unit attached to it, and the last four comments. |
| **CALLS** | Everything still outstanding, newest first. Yours is marked. Tap a call to open it. |
| **UNITS** | Who else is out — callsign, the officers in it, the incident they are on, their status. Partners sharing a callsign are one row, not two. |

Everything on it is sized for a texture rather than a monitor, and no control is smaller than a fingertip. Attach and detach, GPS, Code 6 / On Scene, request backup, and every status in the dock. **This is not the default** — the dashboard shows the Mobile Client unless your owner changes it.

## 🎯 Working it — `/cad`

```bash
/cad
```

The camera leans in to the terminal and the mouse becomes a pointer on the screen. Run `/cad` again — or press **Escape** — to lean back out. It is also bindable in FiveM's own key bindings under **"LACORE: Focus the in-car screen"**, and your owner can rename the command (`VehicleScreen.focusCommand`).

| Input | What it does |
|-------|--------------|
| **Mouse** | Moves the pointer on the terminal and clicks it |
| **Mouse wheel** | Scrolls the list you are pointing at |
| **Escape** | Leans back out |

While you are leaned in, looking around, shooting, the weapon wheel and leaving the vehicle are blocked — the mouse belongs to the terminal, and a click that fires your sidearm instead of attaching you to a call is not a trade anyone wants. **Driving is not blocked.** The camera is attached to the vehicle and rides along through corners and hills, so the screen stays exactly where you put it.

> ⚠️ **There is no typing.** The dashboard terminal is a separate browser handed to the game as a texture, and there is no keyboard channel into it. Anything that needs text — a name query, writing a comment — still wants the real terminal, and the MDT key is unchanged. Those controls are deliberately **absent** from the screen rather than present and dead.

### Two officers, one car

Each occupant gets **their own terminal, signed in as themselves** — the passenger runs the CAD as the passenger, and their status and callsign stay theirs. With `VehicleScreen.sharedCursor` on you also see each other's **pointer**, so "look at this call" has something to point with.

Pointers travel; **clicks do not**. Every terminal is signed in as its own unit, so replaying the passenger's click on the driver's screen would have the driver self-assign to whatever the passenger tapped.

## 🛠 For server owners

### This needs support from the vehicle model

A screen has to be a surface the game can be told to draw on, and that is decided when the model is built — **a script cannot add one**. There are two routes a model can offer, and one command each to find out which you have:

```bash
/lacore screen target
```

```bash
/lacore screen dui
```

`target` registers the model's named render target and draws into it — whatever lights up **is** the render target. `dui` runs the full fallback chain (browser → runtime texture → texture replacement). Both stop on their own after 45 seconds and when you leave the vehicle; `/lacore screen off` stops them early. Run them once per model before adding it to the config.

The **render target wins** wherever a model offers one: it draws on your vehicle only. The texture-replacement fallback works anywhere but swaps the texture for *every* vehicle of that model on your own screen — a parked cruiser nearby then shows your CAD too. Other players still see their own.

### The config

`configs/cfg-vehiclescreen-sh.lua`, one entry per model:

```lua
VehicleScreen.models = {
    ["lapd20fpiu"] = {
        renderTarget = "script_rt_dials_feroci",  -- preferred route
        txd          = "lapd20fpiu",              -- fallback route
        texture      = "mdt_screen",
        cam = {
            pos  = { x = 0.040, y = 0.210, z = 0.680 },
            look = { x = -0.010, y = 1.203, z = 0.575 },
            fov  = 38.000,
        }
    },
}
```

Give both where you have both — the render target is used when the game hands one out, the replacement only if it does not.

### Which terminal is drawn

```lua
VehicleScreen.view = "mdt"
```

| Value | What you get |
|-------|--------------|
| `"mdt"` *(default)* | The LAPD Mobile Client, exactly as the MDT key opens it, filling the screen. |
| `"incar"` | The stripped dashboard terminal — active incident, calls, units, status dock. |
| `"compact"` | Accepted as the old spelling of `"incar"`; behaves identically. |

### Legibility — `VehicleScreen.zoom`

```lua
VehicleScreen.zoom = nil   -- nil = work it out from the screen size
```

The Mobile Client is drawn at monitor pixel sizes and then shown *smaller* than it was drawn (see [why it is bigger](#why-it-is-bigger-than-the-terminal-in-your-window) above), so it is rendered zoomed. Left at `nil` the zoom is derived from `width`/`height` against the terminal's own window size (28vw × 92vh, about 538 × 994 on 1080p): whichever axis runs out first, so the layout is never *smaller* than that window in either direction, and never zoomed below 1 — a wide, short dash panel is left exactly as it was rather than shrunk into confetti.

Set a number to override: `1` is no scaling at all (the raw terminal), `2` is twice the size.

> ⚠️ **Zoom trades content for legibility.** The terminal is being laid out in a smaller box, so the higher it goes the fewer call rows and unit rows fit on screen. It is a zoom, not a font-size setting.

> ℹ️ **Clicks still land.** This is a CSS *zoom*, which is a layout property, so the browser's own hit testing accounts for it — the mouse coordinates `/cad` sends are unaffected and `cursorRect` does not need re-measuring after changing it.

### Glow — `VehicleScreen.brightness`

```lua
VehicleScreen.brightness = 0.6   -- 1.0 = off
```

If your screen looks like a lamp — washed out, blue, blooming into the cabin at night — that is the **vehicle model**, not the terminal.

A vehicle can only offer a render target on a material the engine already knows, and those are **instrument clusters**. The one the shipped Interceptor entry uses is `script_rt_dials_feroci` — GTA's own name for the Feroci dial cluster. Dial clusters are built *self-illuminated* so they glow at night, so anything drawn there is treated as a light source: multiplied by the material's emissive factor, then run through bloom.

It is not the UI being bright. The terminal's own background is `rgb(21, 34, 50)` — luminance **32 out of 255**, all but black — and it still comes out as a lit panel.

Nothing in a resource can reach that emissive factor or the bloom; both live in the model. What can be changed is the input, and since the game *multiplies*, halving what is drawn roughly halves the glow. That is what this setting does.

Set `1.0` to switch it off — worth doing if your model's screen is not emissive, where dimming would only look dark. Lower it further if yours still glows at `0.6`.

> ℹ️ **Clicks are unaffected.** The dimmer takes no pointer events; the mouse belongs to the terminal underneath it.

### Size — give it the *shape*, not just a number

```lua
VehicleScreen.width  = 864
VehicleScreen.height = 1450
```

The texture is stretched onto whatever quad the modder built, so a square image on a portrait tablet arrives squashed — and squashed text reads as *blurry* text, which is why this is easy to miss.

Measure it off `cursorRect` rather than guessing. The default is the Interceptor's tablet: it lands on a 16:9 monitor at 0.265 of the width by 0.791 of the height, so the ratio is `(0.265 × 16) / (0.791 × 9) = 0.596` — hence 864 × 1450. Widescreen dash panels want something more like 1280 × 720. (The `"incar"` view switches to a two-column layout on its own when the screen is wider than it is tall; the Mobile Client keeps its own layout.)

> ⚠️ **This is not the setting that makes text bigger** — `zoom` above is. Raising `width`/`height` gives the browser more pixels to draw the *same* small text into, and then shows them at the same size on your monitor, so it changes nothing you can read and costs performance. It is the main cost of the feature — a real browser rendering continuously — so do not go higher than the screen is worth reading at, and note that `VehicleScreen.passengers` puts one browser in **every occupied seat**, not one per car.

### Aiming it: `/cad adjust`

Two blocks of numbers decide whether the terminal is usable — where the camera goes, and which part of your monitor the screen lands on (`cursorRect`, which is what the mouse is mapped through). Both are per model, and no two models put their screen in the same place. Do not edit them by hand and restart to look. Sit in the vehicle and run:

```bash
/cad adjust
```

**Step 1 — put the screen in frame**

| Input | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **Mouse** | Aim the camera |
| **Arrow keys** | Slide the camera through the cabin |
| **Wheel** | Height · **Shift + Wheel** field of view |
| **Alt** | Move faster |
| **Enter** | Continue to step 2 · **Esc** cancel, nothing is saved |

**Step 2 — mark the screen on your monitor**

Click the **top-left** corner of the terminal, then the **bottom-right**. The frame is drawn live so you can see what you claimed. **Del** starts the rectangle over, **Enter** finishes.

The finished config lines are printed to the **F8 console**, ready to paste back into the file. Nothing is written to disk — a tool that edits your config while you are driving is a tool that eventually edits it wrongly.

### When something is wrong

| Symptom | Almost always |
|---------|---------------|
| The terminal does not react to clicks | `cursorRect` — outside that rectangle no click is sent at all. Fix it with `/cad adjust`, not the camera. |
| Text is small or soft | Raise `VehicleScreen.zoom` — not `width`/`height`, which does not change what you can read. Also check that `width`/`height` match the screen's **shape**: a stretched texture reads as a blurry one. |
| The screen glows / is washed out | The model's screen material is self-illuminated. Lower `VehicleScreen.brightness`. |
| The screen stays the model's own | No entry for that model, you are outside the `audience`, or the model offers neither route — run `/lacore screen target` and `/lacore screen dui`. |
| The camera leans in somewhere else | `cam.pos` / `cam.look` are one measurement of one model. Re-run `/cad adjust`. |

The whole feature sits behind the CAD feature toggle (`Features.cad.enabled` in `configs/cfg-features-sh.lua`) and its own `VehicleScreen.enabled`.
